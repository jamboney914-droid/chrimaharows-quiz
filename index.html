1.1 Origin of the Earth and the Solar System

A. Formation of the Solar System

  • The origin of the Earth is closely linked to the formation of the solar system.
  • The Sun, planets and other smaller bodies formed from a large mass of hot, swirling gases (mainly hydrogen and helium mixed with dust particles).
  • Gravitational pull caused gases and dust to draw together and form a thin spinning disc.
  • The disc split into rings; the central spinning rings formed the Sun, while outer rings formed the eight planets (including Earth).
  • Geologists estimate the Earth was formed 4.6 billion years ago.

💬 In simple words: A huge cloud of hot gas and dust was pulled together by gravity into a spinning disc. The middle became the Sun and the outer rings became the eight planets, including Earth.

B. Members of the Solar System

◆ The Sun

  • Centre of the solar system; composed of extremely hot gases.
  • Provides the gravitational force that holds the solar system together.
  • Ultimate source of heat and light; about 150 million km from Earth.
  • Largest object: comprises 99.8% of the solar system's mass.
⚡
📌 NOTE: Light travels at 300,000 km/second, taking approximately 8 minutes to reach Earth from the Sun.

💬 In simple words: The Sun is the giant hot star in the middle. Its gravity keeps all the planets moving around it and its light and heat give us life. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach us.

  • Small, rocky bodies that orbit the Sun.
  • Found mainly in the Asteroid Belt between Mars and Jupiter.

💬 In simple words: Asteroids are small space rocks. Most of them are in a belt between Mars and Jupiter.

◆ Meteoroids, Meteors & Meteorites

  • Meteoroids: broken pieces of asteroids in space.
  • Meteors: meteoroids that enter Earth's atmosphere and vaporize (appear as 'shooting stars').
  • Meteorites: meteoroids that survive and land on Earth's surface.

💬 In simple words: A meteoroid is a space rock. If it burns up in our air it is a meteor (shooting star). If it reaches the ground it is a meteorite.

1.2 Shape and Movements of the Earth

A. Shape of Earth

  • Earth's shape is an oblate spheroid — slightly flattened at the poles and bulging at the Equator.
  • Cause: gravitational force from Earth's rotation.

💬 In simple words: The Earth is almost a ball, but a little flat at the top and bottom (poles) and a little wide in the middle (Equator).

◆ Evidence of Earth's Spherical Shape

  • The rising Sun is seen earlier in the East — proves Earth is curved.
  • When a ship approaches land, the mast/funnel is seen before the hull.
  • Photographs from space confirm the spheroid shape.

💬 In simple words: Proofs that the Earth is round: the Sun rises earlier in the East, ships appear mast first, and space photos show a round Earth.

B. Movements of the Earth

◆ I. Rotation

⚡
★ Rotation: Movement of the Earth on its own axis.
  • Duration: ~24 hours (one day).
  • Direction: West to East (so the Sun appears to rise in the East and set in the West).
  • Axis tilt: 23½° from perpendicular to orbit.

💬 In simple words: Rotation is the Earth spinning like a top. One spin takes about 24 hours (one day). It spins from west to east, so the Sun seems to rise in the East.

◆ Effects of Rotation

  • Day and Night: half of Earth faces the Sun (day) and half faces away (night).
  • High and Low Tides: result of gravitational pull between the Sun/Moon and the rotating Earth.

💬 In simple words: Because the Earth spins, one side has day and the other side has night. The pull of the Moon and Sun on the oceans makes high and low tides.

◆ II. Revolution

⚡
★ Revolution: Movement of the Earth around the Sun in a fixed elliptical path (orbit).
  • Duration: 365¼ days (one year).
  • Speed: ~109,430 km per hour.

💬 In simple words: Revolution is the Earth travelling around the Sun. One full trip takes 365¼ days, which is one year.

◆ Effects of Revolution

  • Variation in length of day and night from season to season.
Date / EventSignificance
March 21 — Vernal EquinoxDay and night are equal everywhere; Sun directly over Equator
June 21 — Summer SolsticeLongest day in Northern Hemisphere; Sun over Tropic of Cancer
September 23 — Autumnal EquinoxDay and night equal again
December 21 — Winter SolsticeLongest night in Northern Hemisphere; Sun over Tropic of Capricorn
  • Four Seasons: Summer (Kiremt), Autumn (Meher), Winter (Bega), Spring (Belg/Tsedey).

💬 In simple words: Equinox means equal day and night (March and September). Solstice means the longest day or longest night (June and December). Because of the Earth's trip around the Sun we have four seasons.

1.3 External Components & Internal Structure of the Earth

1.3.1 The Four External Components (Spheres)

ComponentDescription
LithosphereThe solid/rock part of Earth; coolest and most rigid; includes mountains, valleys, plateaus
HydrosphereAll water on Earth — oceans, seas, lakes, glaciers; covers ~71% of surface; 97% is ocean water
BiospherePart of Earth where living things exist; from ocean floor to several km into atmosphere
AtmosphereEnvelope of air surrounding Earth; 78% Nitrogen, 21% Oxygen, 1% other gases
⚡
📌 NOTE: The spheres are highly interactive — changes in one sphere affect all others (e.g., rain from atmosphere falls on lithosphere, flows into hydrosphere, supports biosphere).

💬 In simple words: The Earth has four parts: lithosphere (rock), hydrosphere (water), biosphere (living things) and atmosphere (air). They work together, so a change in one changes the others.

1.3.2 Sources of Information about Earth's Interior

◆ A. Direct Sources

  • Mining: Rock samples from depths of 3–4 km (e.g., South African gold mines).
  • Volcanic eruptions: Magma brought to the surface can be analyzed in labs.
  • Deep ocean drilling: The Kola Superdeep Borehole in Russia reached 12 km depth.

◆ B. Indirect Sources

  • Earthquake waves: Travel through Earth and reveal properties of interior layers.
  • Meteorites: Their composition (iron + nickel cores) provides clues about Earth's interior.

💬 In simple words: We cannot dig to the centre of the Earth. Direct sources are things we can touch: mines, volcano magma and deep drilling. Indirect sources are clues: earthquake waves and meteorites.

The Three Types of Rocks

◆ 1. Igneous Rocks

⚡
★ Igneous rocks: Formed when molten rock (magma/lava) cools and solidifies. From Latin 'ignis' = fire.
  • Extrusive (Volcanic): Magma cools on the Earth's surface. Examples: Basalt, Obsidian.
  • Intrusive (Plutonic): Magma cools inside the Earth. Most abundant: Granite.
⚡
📌 NOTE: Magma = molten rock below surface. Lava = magma that reaches the surface. Formed at 60–200 km depth at 900°C–1600°C.

💬 In simple words: Igneous rocks are "fire rocks" made when hot melted rock cools. If it cools outside (lava) it is extrusive, like basalt. If it cools inside the Earth it is intrusive, like granite.

◆ 2. Sedimentary Rocks

⚡
★ Sedimentary rocks: Formed from sediments deposited by erosion agents in rivers, seas, oceans. From Latin 'sedimentum' = settling.
  • Characterized by distinct layers (strata); bottom layer is oldest.
  • Contain fossils — evidence of past life.
  • Source of major energy resources: coal, petroleum, natural gas.

💬 In simple words: Sedimentary rocks are made when sand, mud and shells settle in layers and harden. The lowest layer is the oldest. They hold fossils and fuels like coal and petroleum.

◆ 3. Metamorphic Rocks

⚡
★ Metamorphic rocks: Formed when pre-existing rocks (igneous or sedimentary) change due to heat and pressure inside the crust. From Greek 'meta' (change) + 'morpho' (form).
  • Agents of metamorphism: Heat and Pressure.
  • Example: Limestone (sedimentary) → Marble (metamorphic).

💬 In simple words: Metamorphic means "changed form". Old rocks are changed by heat and pressure, like limestone becoming marble.

1.5 Distribution and Economic Value of Rocks in Ethiopia

Distribution of Rocks in Ethiopia

  • Igneous rocks: Cover large areas of central Ethiopia.
  • Sedimentary rocks: Dominant in eastern Ethiopia.
  • Metamorphic rocks: Found in northern, western, and southern edges of Ethiopia.

💬 In simple words: Central Ethiopia has mostly igneous rocks, the east has sedimentary rocks, and the north, west and south edges have metamorphic rocks.

Economic Importance of Rocks

Rock TypeEconomic Uses
Metamorphic rocksMetallic minerals (gold, nickel, diamond, copper, iron, platinum, lead); decorative and jewelry materials; construction; electrical wire; industrial machinery
Sedimentary rocksMineral fuels (coal, petroleum, natural gas); energy source; writing materials; limestone for construction; gypsum
Igneous rocksBasalt, granite, sand for building and road construction
  • Other uses: Source of fuel, food nutrients (rock salt), soil formation.

💬 In simple words: Rocks are useful: metamorphic rocks give metals and jewelry, sedimentary rocks give fuels, limestone and gypsum, and igneous rocks give building and road stones.

2.1.3 Stages of Human Evolution

◆ Australopithecines (Southern Ape)

  • Earliest human species, appeared ~6 million years ago in Africa.
  • Fossils found in Ethiopia, Kenya, South Africa, and Chad.
  • 'Lucy' (Australopithecus Afarensis) — found in 1974 at Hadar, Afar Region, Ethiopia. May be ancestor of Genus Homo.

💬 In simple words: The first human-like creatures were the Australopithecines (Southern Apes) in Africa. The famous fossil Lucy was found at Hadar in Ethiopia and may be the ancestor of the Genus Homo.

◆ Genus Homo — Three Species

2.2 Trends of World Population

2.2.1 World Population Growth

  • From 1 billion in 1800 to 7.7 billion in 2019; projected to reach 8.5 billion by 2030.
  • This rapid and dramatic growth is called Population Explosion.

◆ Factors Contributing to Rapid Population Growth

  • Advancements in science, agriculture, industry, and medicine.
  • Improved living conditions and public health.
  • Ability to control famine and disease.
  • Lower cost of vaccines, antibiotics, insecticides, and high-yielding seeds.

💬 In simple words: The world population grew very fast, which is called the population explosion. It happened because of better science and medicine, better living conditions, control of hunger and disease, and cheaper vaccines and seeds.

2.2.2 Components of Population Dynamics

◆ A. Fertility

⚡
★ Fertility: The actual reproductive performance of a population.
⚡
★ Birth Rate (BR): Number of live births per 1,000 people per year.
  • BR = (B ÷ P) × 1000 [B = live births; P = mid-year population]
  • Example: If births = 150,800 and population = 50,686,000: BR = (150,800 ÷ 50,686,000) × 1000 = 2.9 per 1,000

💬 In simple words: Fertility is how many children a population actually has. Birth rate = live births divided by the mid-year population, multiplied by 1,000.

◆ Factors Affecting Fertility

  • Low access to contraceptives; poverty; low status of women.
  • Need for child labour; children as symbol of virility; low education.

💬 In simple words: Fertility stays high where families cannot get contraceptives, are poor, women have low status, children are needed to work, children are seen as a sign of strength, and education is low.

◆ B. Mortality

⚡
★ Mortality: The occurrence of death in the human population.
⚡
★ Death Rate (DR): Ratio of total deaths per 1,000 people per year.
  • DR = (D ÷ P) × 1000 [D = deaths; P = mid-year population]
⚡
★ Rate of Natural Increase: Birth Rate minus Death Rate.
⚡
★ Infant Mortality: Death of infants aged below one year.

💬 In simple words: Mortality means deaths. Death rate = deaths divided by the mid-year population, multiplied by 1,000. Natural increase = birth rate minus death rate. Infant mortality means babies who die before age one.

◆ Major Factors for High Death Rate

  • Low standard of living; poor nutrition; lack of health facilities.
  • Civil war; famine; widespread disease.

💬 In simple words: Death rates are high where life is poor, food is short, health care is missing, and where there is war, famine and disease.

◆ C. Migration

⚡
★ Migration: Movement of humans from one place to another.
Push Factors (force people out)Pull Factors (attract people in)
Poor employment, civil war, harsh climateGood jobs, favorable climate, good welfare services
  • Immigration Rate = (Immigrants ÷ Total Destination Population) × 1000
  • Emigration Rate = (Emigrants ÷ Total Origin Population) × 1000
  • NMR = ((Immigrants − Emigrants) ÷ Total Population) × 1000

💬 In simple words: Migration is people moving from place to place. Push factors make people leave (no jobs, war, bad climate). Pull factors attract them (good jobs, climate, services).

2.2.3 Population Characteristics

◆ Age Structure

  • Young: 0–14 years (dependent)
  • Working age: 15–64 years (economically active)
  • Old age: 65+ (dependent)

💬 In simple words: A population is split into three age groups. Children (0–14) and old people (65+) depend on others. The working-age group (15–64) works and supports them.

◆ Population Pyramid

⚡
★ Population Pyramid: Graphic representation of age-sex distribution of a population. Developing countries like Ethiopia have wide bases (large young population).

💬 In simple words: A population pyramid is a graph showing how many males and females are in each age group. A wide base means many children, as in Ethiopia.

◆ Sex Ratio

  • Sex Ratio = (Male Population ÷ Female Population) × 100

💬 In simple words: Sex ratio compares the number of males with the number of females.

◆ Age Dependency Ratio (ADR)

  • ADR = ((Pop. aged 0–14) + (Pop. aged 65+)) ÷ (Pop. aged 15–64) × 100
  • Example: (16,247,000 + 6,217,000) ÷ 27,160,000 × 100 = 82.7% — Ethiopia has a very high ADR.

💬 In simple words: ADR shows how many dependents (children and old people) each group of workers must support. A high ADR means a heavy load on workers, as in Ethiopia.

◆ Population Doubling Time

  • Doubling Time = 70 ÷ Growth Rate (%)

💬 In simple words: Divide 70 by the growth rate to find how long the population takes to double. Example: growth of 2 means about 35 years.

2.3.2 Role of Transportation

  • Provides economic and social opportunities.

💬 In simple words: Good transport helps people reach markets, jobs, hospitals and schools and helps the economy grow. Poor transport costs the economy.

2.3.3 Modes of Transport

◆ 1. Land Transport

  • Railway: Transports passengers and heavy bulk goods; Addis Ababa–Djibouti railway = 784 km; Addis Ababa Light Rail (first in East Africa, launched Sept 2015) = 31.6 km.
  • Pipelines: Specialized; transports liquids and gases (e.g., oil).

💬 In simple words: Land transport has three kinds: roads (oldest, door to door), railways (heavy goods and many passengers) and pipelines (oil and gas only).

  • Always use sidewalks; cross at designated crossings; follow traffic signals.

◆ Key Rules for Drivers

  • Never drink and drive; never break red signals; drive within speed limits; follow road signs.

◆ Impacts of Road Traffic Accidents

  • Loss of lives; injuries and permanent disability; increased poverty.
  • Economic losses: treatment costs, lost productivity, loss of skilled labour.
  • More than half of road deaths involve pedestrians, cyclists, and motorcyclists.

💬 In simple words: Walkers must use sidewalks and crossings and obey signals. Drivers must never drink and drive, never break red lights and must obey speed limits and signs. Accidents cause death, injury, poverty and big economic losses.
